Method

Nothing is estimated here. Values are stored as the source publishes them. The site calculates three things from them: India's share of world GDP at market rates (India divided by the world total), its rank among countries (regional and income aggregates excluded), and the averages and ratios quoted in chart titles.

Years are fiscal years. India's national accounts run from April to March. The World Bank and the IMF file each fiscal year under the calendar year in which it begins, so 2024 means April 2024 to March 2025. State accounts are labelled in full, as 2024-25. Quarterly figures are placed in their calendar quarter: the first quarter of fiscal 2025-26 appears as April to June 2025.

Dollars. "Current US$" converts at each year's market exchange rate, so those series move with the rupee as well as with output. Constant-price series remove inflation. Purchasing power parity adjusts for India's lower prices and makes India look about two and a half times larger than market rates do.

Projections. Only IMF series carry projections. They are drawn dotted or faded, marked in tables and never mixed into "latest" figures.

Chart titles are generated. Each title states a fact computed from the data at build time, so a title cannot drift out of date when the numbers are revised.

Known weaknesses

Two GDP base years. The statistics ministry moved the national accounts to a 2022-23 base in 2026. Quarterly growth here uses the new base where it exists and the 2011-12 base before that; the join is not strictly comparable. State accounts are still on the 2011-12 base.

Consumer prices by group stop in December 2025. The ministry moved to a 2024 base in January 2026. The headline rate here is current, joining the BIS series to the ministry's new index for the latest months; the food, fuel and housing breakdowns are still on the old base.

Base-year joins. Industrial production and wholesale prices use the 2022-23 base from April 2024 and the 2011-12 base before it. Growth rates either side of the join are not strictly comparable.

UPI figures run about two months behind. They come from the Reserve Bank's monthly Bulletin, because the payments corporation that publishes them first blocks automated collection. The numbers are the same; they arrive later.

Monthly government accounts are provisional. Single-month figures are the change in the published year-to-date total, so a correction to an earlier month lands in the month it is made. Gross GST collections begin in April 2020; state figures begin in July 2017.

State budgets are unaudited and uneven. They come from the national auditor's Monthly Key Indicators. States publish at different speeds, so the latest month differs from state to state. Delhi, Goa and Puducherry list no files. Some states publish scanned pages a script cannot read (Uttarakhand, parts of Meghalaya), and a document is rejected when its own figures do not add up, which leaves Kerala and Sikkim behind. State debt is not covered; the Reserve Bank's study of state budgets, which has it, blocks automated collection.

Rail and road figures are annual. They come from the Economic Survey, updated each January, and cover five years for the railways. The railways and the roads ministry publish monthly loading and highway construction in forms a script cannot read, so there is no monthly series, and no yearly series of highway kilometres built: only the network's length and the ministry's period averages.

The nowcast and the activity index are this site's own calculations, marked experimental wherever they appear. The nowcast's track record is published beside it.

Sources disagree on rank. The World Bank and the IMF use slightly different exchange rates and vintages, so India's place among economies can differ by one position between pages. Each page says which source it uses.

Jobs data are thin. Long-run unemployment and participation rates are International Labour Organization model estimates. India's own monthly labour survey begins only in 2025.

Poverty and literacy are measured in survey years only, sometimes a decade apart, and survey methods have changed between rounds.

Not available in this build: state budgets: no readable accounts for Delhi, Goa, Puducherry.

Updates

One command rebuilds everything from the sources: npm run data, then npm run build. A scheduled job can run both daily. Before building, a check script confirms that the essential series are present, recent and within plausible ranges, and stops the build if they are not.
